# ECON1626 Assessment 2: AI and the Labour Market Policy Proposal

This repository contains my policy proposal for the **Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR)**. I recommend support to help clerical and administrative workers adjust as generative AI takes over routine cognitive tasks.

I discuss worker support alongside the **Australian Standards for AI**. The Prime Minister announced these standards on 15 July 2026. They are being developed by the new Office of AI in the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet (PM&C), and legislation is expected in early 2027.

## How to read the brief

1. Start with the **Executive summary** for the recommendation.
2. **Sections 1–2** set out why AI is changing clerical work and who bears the cost, using ABS, JSA and Productivity Commission data.
3. **Section 3** compares three options against explicit criteria in a matrix.
4. **Section 4** sets out the recommended $420m package, with its timeline, responsible agencies, costs and KPIs.
5. **Section 5** lists the main risks and how to mitigate them.

## Evidence base

The main sources are:

- ABS Labour Force (Detailed) and Employee Earnings data
- Jobs and Skills Australia, *Our Gen AI Transition* (2025)
- Productivity Commission, *Harnessing data and digital technology* (2025)
- peer-reviewed research on AI and tasks, such as Acemoglu & Restrepo (2019) and Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (2025).

Every statistic is cited in the reference list. Costs and targets are planning assumptions, explained in `costing.md`. The proposal distinguishes descriptive employment evidence from causal estimates. Pilot sizes and performance targets are policy assumptions, with their limitations explained in the costing appendix.
