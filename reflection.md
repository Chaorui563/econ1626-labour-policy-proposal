# AI Use Reflection

I chose to focus on clerical workers and combine support before redundancy with assistance after job loss. My reasoning was that retraining within a firm cannot help someone already displaced. I used AI assistance for research, drafting and revision, and asked Codex to review the proposal against the assessment requirements and lecture material.

A useful example was the Stanford employment finding. My interpretation depended on the comparison behind the 19% figure, not simply its size. The review helped make that distinction explicit: employment was below the level implied by less-exposed workers' trends. This does not establish that AI caused the gap. For my proposal, the finding supports monitoring and targeted support, rather than a claim that widespread job losses are inevitable.

I also accepted a budget correction. Option B began halfway through the first financial year, but its costs covered four full years. Revising participant numbers reduced the package from $450m to $420m.

These checks showed me that a source can be credible while a sentence overstates what it establishes. AI helped revise the analysis, but its confident wording could also conceal assumptions. I remain responsible for distinguishing evidence from interpretation and checking whether my recommendation follows. AI also helped edit this reflection.
